Building living hearts, one organoid at a time.

I'm Young Hyun Kim, a graduate researcher at the Biological Systems Engineering Laboratory, Sejong University. I work on human iPSC-derived chamber-specific cardiac organoids, and I build the computational tools and instrumentation needed to measure how they beat.

My work sits at the intersection of stem cell biology, tissue engineering, and quantitative imaging analysis — differentiating cells, building tissue, and then writing the software that tells us whether it's actually working.

What I work on

Research at a glance

A quick tour of the three things I spend most of my time on. Full detail lives on the Research and Publications pages.

🫀

Chamber-specific cardiac organoids

Differentiating hiPSCs into ventricular and atrial cardiomyocytes (~97.5% cTNT⁺ efficiency) and fabricating 2D monolayers and 3D organoids for maturation and disease modeling.

📈

iCAMAnalyzer

A custom MATLAB App Designer tool I built for calcium-imaging analysis — transient kinetics, beat-to-beat analysis, activation mapping, and conduction-velocity estimation, validated against synthetic ground truth (~7% error).

🔬

Instrumentation & pipelines

Streamlit/Python pipelines for multi-organoid imaging, a 3D-printed micropillar culture platform, and a bio-synchronous electromechanical stimulator for 3D cardiac tissue.
