Research Starter Kit

Practical notes for starting experimental research

Research is full of things that are rarely taught explicitly: how to organize an experiment, what to record, how to troubleshoot failed protocols, how to turn raw data into a figure, and how to communicate results clearly.

I built this page as a collection of practical notes I wish I had when I first started working in a lab.

01 · Start the Experiment

Start with a system you can understand

Good experiments are easier to interpret when the variables, controls, and expected outcomes are clear before the experiment begins.

I try to define the question first, then work backward to the measurements, controls, and experimental conditions needed to answer it.

What I think about before starting

  • What is the biological question?
  • What variable am I actually changing?
  • What is the appropriate control?
  • What result would support or challenge the hypothesis?
  • What metadata will I need later during analysis?

Topics: Experimental planning · Controls · Pilot studies · Lab notes · Sample tracking · Troubleshooting

Notes I plan to share

How I plan pilot experiments before scaling up

Start small, identify the unstable variables, and only scale once the workflow is reproducible.

What I record even when an experiment fails

Failed experiments are still useful if the conditions and deviations are documented clearly.

Why I decide the analysis plan before collecting data

Knowing what will eventually be measured helps prevent experiments that produce interesting images but weak quantitative conclusions.

02 · Read & Synthesize

Reading papers is different from collecting papers

The goal is not to accumulate PDFs. It is to understand how studies relate to one another, where they disagree, and what question remains unanswered.

When I read literature for a new project, I try to extract the experimental logic rather than only the conclusion.

What I usually extract

  • Research question
  • Model system
  • Key experimental variables
  • Methods that determine the conclusion
  • Major readouts
  • Limitations
  • What I would test next

Topics: Literature review · Research gaps · Reference management · Comparison tables · Experimental design

Notes I plan to share

How I read a paper when entering a new field

I usually begin with the figures and research question before reading the paper line by line.

Building a literature comparison table

Organizing model systems, conditions, and readouts side by side makes research gaps much easier to see.

How review papers help me design experiments

Reviews are useful for mapping a field, but experimental papers are where I look for the details needed to build a protocol.

From literature gap to experimental question

A useful research gap should lead to a testable question, not simply a statement that something has not been studied.

03 · Analyze & Visualize

Raw data should lead to a reproducible result

Analysis is where an experimental observation becomes evidence.

I use existing tools when they answer the question well, but when the available workflow is too manual or does not capture the signal I need, I build custom analysis pipelines.

Tools I use

ImageJ

Quick image inspection, ROI-based measurements, preprocessing, and basic biological image analysis.

GraphPad Prism

Statistical analysis and clear biological data visualization.

MATLAB

Custom analysis applications, image and video processing, signal analysis, and rapid prototyping.

Python

Automated analysis pipelines, data processing, image analysis, and scalable workflow development.

Adobe Illustrator & Inkscape

Figure assembly, scientific illustration, and visual organization of complex experimental concepts.

Topics: Image analysis · Signal processing · Statistics · Automation · Figure design · Validation

Notes I plan to share

How I go from raw video to a quantitative cardiac readout

A practical workflow from ROI definition to signal extraction, normalization, feature detection, and visualization.

Why custom analysis tools need validation

A tool is only useful if its output can be compared against known values, manual measurements, or simulated ground truth.

When I use MATLAB, Python, ImageJ, or Prism

Different tools are useful at different stages; the goal is not to force every problem into one software package.

How I organize figures before writing the manuscript

I often build the figure logic first because it reveals whether the experimental story is actually coherent.

04 · Write & Communicate

A clear figure often comes before a clear paragraph

Scientific writing becomes much easier when the logic of the experiment is already organized. I usually think in terms of:

Question → Experiment → Result → Interpretation

and try to make each figure communicate one main point.

Topics: Figure-first writing · Thesis structure · Posters · Presentations · Manuscripts · Advisor communication

Notes I plan to share

How I turn experiments into a thesis story

A thesis should not be a chronological record of everything that happened in the lab. It should be a structured argument.

Why I often plan figures before writing Results

If a figure cannot communicate the result clearly, the underlying analysis or experimental question may still need refinement.

How I prepare research presentations

I try to make each slide answer one question and create a reason for the audience to care about the next slide.

How I decide what belongs in a schematic figure

A good schematic simplifies the system without distorting the biology.

Why I Made This Page

When I first started research, many of the hardest parts were not the techniques themselves. It was knowing how to organize the work, how much detail to record, how to recover from failed experiments, how to choose an analysis method, and how to turn months of experiments into a clear scientific story.

I learned most of these things gradually through trial and error. This page is my attempt to organize those lessons into practical notes for students who are just beginning experimental research.

I am still learning too, so I treat these as working notes rather than universal rules.
