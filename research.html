From Chamber-Specific Cardiac Organoids to Electromechanical Function

I engineer chamber-specific cardiac organoids from hiPSCs and use quantitative phenotyping to determine how cardiac identity is reflected in calcium dynamics, electrical propagation, and contraction.

I am now interested in what physical and mechanical environments drive those functional phenotypes.

01 — Build: Chamber-Specific Cardiac Organoids

hiPSC · Embryoid body formation · Wnt / RA modulation · 3D directed differentiation

Why two chambers?

Atrial and ventricular myocardium differ in molecular identity, calcium handling, electrophysiology, and pharmacological response. I therefore generated atrial- and ventricular-like organoids to ask whether chamber identity could be directed in a 3D hiPSC model—and whether those identities would translate into measurable functional differences.

Model generation. hiPSC → embryoid body formation → 3D cardiac differentiation → atrial- / ventricular-like organoids.

Validation. Chamber-associated molecular identity was assessed using NR2F2 / IRX4, MLC2a/v, Cx40/43, and sarcomeric organization.

Future research focus

How Physical Context Shapes Cardiac Phenotype

I am interested in how ECM confinement, suspension culture, and fluid shear during organoid formation reshape tissue organization, chamber phenotype, and electromechanical function.

One question I am especially interested in is how the mechanical history of organoid formation reshapes later electromechanical phenotype.

Building on my chamber-specific organoid work, I want to ask whether physical culture environments remodel not only maturation, but also chamber-dependent electromechanical behavior.
