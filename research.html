M.S. thesis research

Build, Measure, and Scale

My thesis centered on a chamber-specific cardiac organoid platform and a quantitative framework for linking molecular identity with electromechanical function.

M.S. in Integrative Biological Sciences and Industry · Sejong University · August 2026 · Advisor: Keel Yong Lee, Ph.D.

01 — Build: Chamber-Specific Cardiac Organoids

hiPSC · Embryoid body formation · Wnt / RA modulation · 3D directed differentiation

Why two chambers?

Atrial and ventricular myocardium differ in molecular identity, calcium handling, electrophysiology, and pharmacological response. I therefore generated atrial- and ventricular-like organoids to ask whether chamber identity could be directed in a 3D hiPSC model—and whether those identities would translate into measurable functional differences.

Model generation. hiPSC → embryoid body formation → 3D cardiac differentiation → atrial- / ventricular-like organoids.

Chamber identity. Molecular identity was assessed using chamber-associated markers including NR2F2 / IRX4, MLC2a/v, Cx40/43, and sarcomeric organization.

Mechanical — Motion Analysis

Label-free readout

Bright-field motion analysis provides a complementary mechanical readout without fluorescent labeling.

  • Beating rate

Physiological validation

Chamber-dependent pharmacological response

Carbachol and isoproterenol produced chamber-dependent responses, providing functional evidence that supported the atrial- and ventricular-like phenotypes.

03 — Scale

Parallel Organoid Phenotyping

I extended the platform toward parallel acquisition and higher-throughput analysis while keeping the chamber-specific functional readouts central.

3D-printed platform

Micropillar Culture Platform

Custom SLA-fabricated micropillar arrays were designed for multi-organoid culture, positioning, and imaging.

SLA fabrication · Multi-organoid layout · Imaging compatibility

Parallel acquisition

9-Organoid Recording

Parallel acquisition across nine organoids served as a proof-of-concept for scaling functional phenotyping beyond one sample at a time.

Parallel recording · Batch comparison · Higher-throughput phenotyping

Potential application. This framework could support higher-throughput functional screening and cardiotoxicity assessment.

Ongoing engineering projects

Ongoing Engineering Projects

Complementary engineering projects that extend my cardiac tissue research across stimulation, imaging, simulation, and mechanically defined culture environments.

Stimulation

Electromechanical Stimulation Platform

I am developing a culture platform that combines cyclic mechanical stretch with electrical pacing for engineered cardiac tissues. The project integrates device development with culture-condition testing to examine how external physical cues influence tissue organization and function.

Mechanical stretch · Electrical pacing · Device integration · Culture-condition testing

Imaging

Dual-Camera Functional Imaging System

I am building a synchronized fluorescence and brightfield imaging system to capture calcium dynamics and tissue motion from the same cardiac sample. The goal is to integrate optical readouts of excitation and contraction within a single acquisition workflow.

Fluorescence · Brightfield · Ca²⁺ imaging · Motion analysis · Optical synchronization

Simulation

Cardiac Electromechanical Simulation

I am developing computational models that connect calcium-wave propagation with tissue contraction and structural deformation. Current models include Ca²⁺-driven contraction, muscle-thin-film bending, and tissue-mold pillar deflection in response to engineered muscle force.

Ca²⁺ propagation · Excitation–contraction coupling · MTF bending · Pillar deflection · Computational modeling

Culture environment

Spinner-Based Mechanical Conditioning

I am investigating how spinner-based suspension culture influences organoid productivity while exposing developing tissues to externally imposed mechanical stress. This project connects culture scale-up with my broader interest in how physical environments shape cardiac phenotype.

Spinner culture · Suspension environment · Shear exposure · Productivity · Mechanical stress

Future research direction

How Physical Context Shapes Cardiac Phenotype

I am interested in how mechanical and microenvironmental conditions during tissue formation reshape later cardiac organization and electromechanical function. Building on my chamber-specific organoid work, I want to move from describing functional differences to identifying the mechanisms that drive them.
