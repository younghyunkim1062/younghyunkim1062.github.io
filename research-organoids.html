Chamber-specific cardiac organoids

Do atrial- and ventricular-like cardiac tissues develop distinct functional phenotypes? I combined organoid generation, tissue characterization, and optical measurements to examine that question.

Sejong University · Biological Systems Engineering LaboratoryThesis defense · June 2026

01 · Building the model

From hiPSCs to 3D cardiac tissue.

I developed atrial- and ventricular-like organoids through Wnt and retinoic-acid modulation, with optimization of cell seeding and aggregation. This created a platform for comparing chamber-associated phenotypes across complementary readouts.

02 · Characterizing tissue identity

Structure and markers provide context for function.

I used qRT-PCR and flow cytometry in monolayer cultures, alongside immunofluorescence in 3D tissues. cTnT and α-actinin staining supported cardiomyocyte identity and revealed organized sarcomeric structures.

MLC2a/MLC2v and connexin staining provided additional chamber-associated information. Isoform co-expression and incomplete quantitative time-course data limit conclusions about chamber purity and maturation.

03 · Functional observations

Chamber-associated differences depend on the readout.

Calcium kinetics

Atrial-like organoids showed faster, shorter calcium transients, while ventricular-like organoids showed more prolonged transients in the evaluated recordings. Transient duration, decay kinetics, and waveform shape provided complementary descriptors.

Tissue motion

Label-free brightfield analysis captured differences in beating rate and contraction profiles. The study also examined paced responses. These are image-derived motion measurements; direct force estimation requires an appropriate mechanical calibration.

Calcium-derived propagation: The study did not detect a statistically significant group difference in calcium-derived propagation velocity. This does not establish equivalence. The measurement describes calcium-signal propagation, rather than a direct electrical conduction measurement.

04 · Scaling the measurement

From one tissue to multiple organoids.

A micropillar-assisted proof of concept extended the workflow to multiple tissues, including a nine-organoid brightfield analysis. The platform connects tissue positioning with region-based extraction of functional signals.

Parallel analysis here refers to multiple organoids. Calcium and brightfield acquisition were separate in this study.

Interpretation & next steps

What these observations establish—and what comes next.

The study supports readout-dependent functional differences between the evaluated atrial- and ventricular-like models. It does not establish fully mature or pure chamber identities across cell lines. Sample size, the use of one hiPSC line, incomplete marker time courses, and incomplete chamber-paired pharmacology limit generalization.

These findings motivate synchronized calcium–brightfield imaging to resolve timing within the same beat, followed by controlled perturbations to test the relationship between calcium handling and contraction.
