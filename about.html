Maker · Educator · Bioengineer

From moving robots
to beating tissues.

I’m Young Hyun Kim. I enjoy turning ideas into things that move—and helping others understand how they work. Before working with living cells, I built educational robots, drawing inspiration from museums, art exhibitions, and musicals.

Years later, a biohybrid fish powered by cardiac tissue caught my attention: living cells were doing the job I had once given to motors. That connection drew me toward cardiac tissue engineering. Today, I bring the same curiosity to building tissues, developing tools, and learning alongside others.

Make something. Learn how it works. Share what you discover.

My path

Turning Points

From building robots to engineering living systems.

  1. 2017–2019 · Robotics & education

    Learning by making.

    At CreArtBot, I built interactive robots and taught robotics and STEAM workshops. Turning an idea into something that moved taught me to build, test, and explain a system.

    Explore the making projects →
  2. Oct 2022–Aug 2023 · Undergraduate research assistant

    From machines to living systems.

    As one of the lab’s first three members, I established its initial protocol for making cardiac organoids from human iPSCs. I also built 3D skeletal muscle tissues from C2C12 cells and applied cyclic stretch to study maturation.

    See the cardiac models →
  3. August 2023 · Sejong University

    Bachelor’s graduation

    B.Eng. in Integrative Bioscience and Biotechnology. After exploring robotics, education, and undergraduate research, I continued into graduate study to work more deeply with living systems.

  4. Sep 2023–Aug 2026 · Graduate research assistant

    Building the tissue and the tools.

    For my master’s thesis, I generated atrial- and ventricular-like cardiac organoids and developed iCAMAnalyzer to compare their calcium signals and contractile motion.

    Explore the analysis platform →
  5. August 2026 · Sejong University

    Master’s graduation

    M.S. in Integrative Bioscience and Biotechnology. My thesis brought together cardiac organoid culture and quantitative analysis—building both the tissues and the tools to study their function.

Next chapter

Ph.D. training

I hope to use engineered cardiac models and quantitative physiology to study the mechanisms that shape normal and disease-associated cardiac function.

Making & sharing

Before the Bench

Teaching robotics taught me that an explanation clear to me might not be clear to someone else. I built examples alongside learners and explained how sensors, controllers, and motors worked together. Training with BirdBrain Technologies in the United States helped me adapt my teaching to people with different levels of experience.

That experience still shapes how I mentor. As a teaching assistant and research mentor, I help students work through unfamiliar techniques, ask questions, and develop experiments and presentations of their own.
